# b2-4: Graded timing and accommodations

**SPEC rows:** AC-47, AC-48   **Model:** claude-haiku-4-5   **Commits:** (TBD after final commit)

## Decision

**Problem:** Graded work needs reliable duration measurement even when the device is offline or the clock drifts. Learners with disabilities need customizable time accommodations.

**Options considered:**
1. Trust only device wall-clock time: Simple but breaks if clock drifts or is manually adjusted.
2. Use hub-signed times when available, fall back to monotonic: Secure and resilient. Detects clock skew.
3. Allow arbitrary time multipliers: Risk of abuse; need bounds.

**Choice:** 
- Hub-signed start/end times are authoritative when present (option 2).
- Fall back to monotonic duration with a flag if hub times are missing (offline attempt).
- Detect clock skew when device wall-clock differs from monotonic by >60s.
- Time multiplier defaults to 1 and is clamped to 1–3 for accommodations.

**Why:** 
SPEC §4.26 and D-27 mandate hub-signed times for grading security. Monotonic time is more reliable than wall-clock because it's not affected by NTP, manual adjustment, or timezone changes. Clamping the multiplier prevents misuse while still supporting legitimate accommodations (1.5–2× for learning disabilities, 3× in rare cases).

## What a learner will trip on

- **Threshold confusion:** 60-second threshold is exact; 60.000s does not flag, 60.001s does. Boundary condition testing is important.
- **Monotonic semantics:** "Monotonic duration" means elapsed time from a process-local clock (not wall time). Learners may confuse this with real-time clocks.
- **Accommodation in isolation:** The time multiplier is applied here, but the base time is determined by the attempt's scoring system; these are separate concerns.
- **Device clock skew in offline mode:** Even when offline (hub times unavailable), we still detect and flag device clock skew. This signals a problem to be investigated.
